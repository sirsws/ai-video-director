# Rain at the pavilion

**A worked design example.** This page demonstrates the method; it is not a measured or verified video-generation result. For actual published work, see the [film showcase](../README.md#films-and-the-decisions-behind-them).

## The brief

A 15-second, 9:16 rainy-night short. One traveler enters a wooden pavilion, closes an umbrella, and shakes off the rain. No fight. No dialogue. Deliver a prompt for the user to generate.

## What makes it a scene

The experience is the transition from exposure to shelter. The action, framing, and sound all make that change visible and audible.

- **Space:** establish the pavilion, wet approach, threshold, dry bench, and outer drain in one readable view.
- **Action:** stop inside the eaves, tip the umbrella toward the outside, close it, then shake it over the drain. Each step has a destination and an order.
- **Camera:** keep the traveler's hands and the whole umbrella visible. A modest following move brings us to the threshold, then settles.
- **Sound:** open rain gives way to rain hitting the roof and water outside. The canopy folding and two short shakes become distinct close sounds.
- **Ending:** the umbrella rests by the outer post. A short still moment lets the shelter register.

## Copyable video prompt

```text
15 seconds, vertical 9:16. A rainy night at a small wooden pavilion beside a stone path. A dry bench sits along the back wall; a shallow drain runs outside the entrance. Warm light under the eaves separates the dry interior from the blue-gray rain. One adult traveler in a dark raincoat carries an open, plain umbrella. No dialogue or fighting.

Begin just behind and to one side of the traveler, wide enough to show the umbrella, both hands, the threshold, and the pavilion ahead. Follow the final steps along the wet path. Keep the pavilion's entrance and the outer drain visible as the traveler arrives. Settle the camera near the entrance rather than continuing around the body.

The traveler stops under the eaves and tips the umbrella outward over the drain, away from the dry bench. One hand steadies the handle while the other releases and folds the canopy. After it is fully closed, the traveler gives it two short downward shakes over the drain. Water scatters outside the threshold. The traveler rests the closed umbrella against the outer post and releases it only once it is stable. Hold briefly on the sheltered figure while rain continues beyond the eaves.

Start with broad rain on the path. As the traveler enters, let the roof rain become the surrounding sound and the outdoor rain recede. Hear the folding canopy, two brief splashes, and the umbrella touching the post. No music, speech, or subtitles.
```

## What to check in the actual result

1. Does the approach lead into the established entrance without a spatial jump?
2. Do the hands, canopy, closure, and two shakes remain readable?
3. Does the water land outside, leaving the dry bench consistent?
4. Does the umbrella stay closed and remain stable when released?
5. Does the audio actually change at the move into shelter? If the host cannot listen, ask for the user's listening feedback.

If the umbrella is already closed at the start, correct the starting state or reference input. If the bench gets wet, correct the direction of the action. If the camera hides the closure, adjust the viewing side or framing. A request for local repair does not require replacing the central event.

The task can start without a reference image when no specific identity or costume is required. Use references when they carry a real requirement, and adapt the wording to the actual platform's supported input and duration.
